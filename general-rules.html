Chapter I

General Rules

To survive in the glorious city of Lund, you need to know the rules of Lund.

1.1

Virtues & Guard

  • Virtues can be reduced and raised in a number of ways, but can never go higher than 19 or lower than 0.

Every character is defined by three Virtues, each rolled 1d6 + 1d12, plus a separate Guard score rolled 1d6.

VirtueNature
VigorStrong Body, Sturdy Bones, Mighty Heart
GraceKeen Thought, Swift Reflex, Flawless Motion
SpiritComposed Mind, Striking Presence, Guarded Soul
Guard

Guard is a pool of protection rolled separately (1d6) at character creation. Incoming damage is subtracted from Guard before it touches a Virtue score — once Guard is depleted, hits land in full. Fatekin cannot disengage from enemies if they lack guard.

Armor — worn plate, a raised shield, a warded charm — reduces Damage directly, before it ever reaches Guard. See Attacking, below.

1.2

Virtue Loss

Virtues may be reduced by means other than Damage — this is Virtue Loss. Beyond the situations listed elsewhere in these rules, characters may suffer Virtue Loss as the cost or consequence of an action, detailed case by case where it applies.

Victims are only Slain or Mortally Wounded if the Virtue was reduced by Damage — never as a result of Virtue Loss.

At ZeroEffect
Vigor 0Exhausted — cannot Attack if you have moved this turn
Grace 0Your attacks are Impaired, rolling a single d4 only
Spirit 0Exposed — your Guard is set to 0
1.3

Saves & Checks

Saves

To pass a Save, roll a d20 equal to or below the relevant Virtue. Failure means negative consequences — not always a failed action.

Checks

To pass a Check, roll a d20 equal to or below the relevant Virtue. When performing Opposed Checks, the lower check wins — but only if it's equal to or below the relevant Virtue. Those with the most to lose from the check's outcome win ties.

Advantage means rolling 2d20 and keeping the lower result; Disadvantage means rolling 2d20 and keeping the higher result — the inverse of how these usually work, since rolling low is what you want here.

1.4

Turns

A turn is enough time to Move and perform an Action, in either order. Players always take their turn first, then enemies act.

A move typically covers about 10m of ground. Fatekin who are in touch range of an enemy cannot move away unless they have guard remaining, or an ally is next to them to assist with their retreat.

Common Actions include making an Attack, casting a Spell (Chapter III), or Aiding an ally. There's no individual initiative roll — the party moves as one side, then the opposition responds as the other.

1.5

Attacking

Take the Attack dice noted on your weapon(s), shield, and any bonuses granted. Go through the following steps in order:

  1. All combatants attacking the same target roll their dice at the same time.
  2. If the target or their nearby allies can Deny, they may use it.
  3. Attackers may perform Gambits.
  4. Take the highest die remaining.
  5. Add any extra Damage from attackers who are Bolstering the Attack.
  6. Subtract the target's total Armor score.
  7. The Attack causes that much Damage.
Worked Example

A Fatekin with a mace (d8) and shield (d4) is aided by an ally with two daggers (d6 each). They roll d8, d4, and 2d6. The dice show 7, 3, 1, and 5. The highest die is 7. The 5 is spent on a Gambit to Bolster the Damage to 8. The target has Armor 2 (helm and shield), reducing the Damage to 6.

Impaired attacks roll a single d4 only, and cannot gain bonus dice or benefit from Feats.

Blast attacks target everybody in their area, rolling each separately.

Enhanced attacks gain a bonus die. Whatever grants the Enhancement will typically specify which die — if it doesn't, the bonus die is a d8.

1.6

Gambits

Attackers may discard any number of Attack dice showing 4 or higher to perform Gambits, each causing one of the effects below. The affected foe must be a target of the original attack.

For Gambits other than Bolster and Move, the target receives a Vigor Save to ignore the effect.

  • Bolster the Attack for +1 total Damage
  • Move after the Attack, even if you already moved or are unable to move
  • Repel a foe away from you
  • Stop a foe from moving next turn
  • Impair a foe's weapon or spellcasting next turn
  • Trap a shield or silence a spellcaster until your next turn
  • Dismount a foe or disable a vehicle until your next turn
  • Other effect of a similar level of impact

If a die of 8 or higher is used in any Attack, including ranged weapons and spells, a Strong Gambit is performed instead, and the attacker chooses one of the following:

  • No Save is granted to the target
  • A greater effect, such as disarming an item, breaking a wooden shield or weapon, or removing a helm — this does not increase the Damage from Bolster
1.7

Feats

These special actions known by Fatekin can be performed as described. Using a Feat risks the Fatekin becoming Fatigued, which prevents them from using further Feats until they rest. Each Feat can only be used once per Attack by each combatant involved.

Most Feats carry an affinity toward one suit of the Arcana — Deny and Focus are universal, learned by any Fatekin regardless of leaning.

Deny

Universal

Rebuff an attack before it lands. Use after an Attack roll against you or an ally within arm's reach — discard one Attack die from the roll.

Pass a Grace Save or become Fatigued

Focus

Universal

Create an opening to exploit. Use after rolling an Attack — perform a Gambit without spending a die.

Pass a Spirit Save or become Fatigued

Divine Smite

Suit of Pentacles

Use before making any melee Attack that lacks the Blast property. That Attack gains +1d12.

Pass a Vigor Save or become Fatigued

Cleave

Suit of Swords

Use before making any melee Attack. That Attack gains Blast.

Pass a Vigor Save or become Fatigued

Masterstrike

Suit of Swords

Use before making any Attack that lacks the Blast property. That Attack ignores Armor.

Pass a Vigor Save or become Fatigued

Morph

Suit of Wands

Use when any spell you can see is cast. You may modify that spell in one of the following ways:

  • Blast ignores allies of the caster
  • Remove or add Blast to the spell
  • Change the Virtue cost of the spell for the caster
  • Change the spell's damage type
  • Add or remove a target from a targeted spell
  • Cast the spell without sound or gesture

Pass a Spirit Save or become Fatigued

Humiliate

Suit of Cups

Use when dealing Damage. Choose which Virtue is damaged on the target.

Pass a Spirit Save or become Fatigued

Ruse

Suit of Cups

Use before making any Attack. Make two attacks and pick one to use.

Pass a Grace Save or become Fatigued

1.8

Spellcasting

Casting a Spell

As an action, a Fatekin can cast a spell. Before casting completes, they may use the Morph Feat if they have it. After the spellcaster applies their own Morph effect, other valid Morph effects may be applied by others. A Morph effect cannot be applied twice, and if two effects would cancel each other out, they simply do.

Once a spell is successfully cast and any Morph effects are resolved, the spell's casting cost is deducted from the caster's corresponding Virtue. By default this is always Spirit, unless the spell states otherwise or a Morph effect has adjusted it.

Every spell has a Virtue cost. Some are free, but a typical cost is either 2, 4, or 8 points of the relevant Virtue.

Overflow

If a casting cost exceeds the caster's remaining casting Virtue (typically Spirit), the excess is always taken from Vigor instead. If Vigor is itself the spellcasting Virtue being used when that excess applies, the caster immediately suffers a mortal wound.

Spells typically take effect immediately and rarely allow a Save — only when the effect would leave the target disabled, helpless, or wholly ineffective.

Qualities of Spells

Range — spells typically use one of five ranges:

  • Self
  • Touch (within a meter)
  • Medium-Range (within 10 meters)
  • Long-Range (within 100 meters)
  • Extreme-Range (within 500 meters)

Attack, Save, or Direct Damage — spells that deal damage usually do so through an Attack, following the same rules as Attacking, but using the dice the spell provides.

Some spells deal Direct damage to a Virtue instead. This isn't an Attack — it simply reduces the target's Virtue as stated, ignoring both Guard and Armor.

Some spells call for a Save when cast; the defender saves against their own Virtue score.

Targets — a targeted spell affects one creature, and can be Morphed to add or remove targets. Spells cast on Self are not considered to target a creature. When Morph is used to add a target, the new target must be within the spell's original range.

Blast — a spell with Blast can affect more than one creature at once. How many creatures a Blast can affect is up to the GM, but a good baseline: the size of the die used by the spell sets the maximum number of normal-sized creatures the Blast can contain.

Example

A Blast spell dealing 2d8 Fire damage at Medium-Range could affect up to 8 normal-sized creatures.

When Blast is added to a spell with no die size, its extent is left to the GM's judgment.

When casting a Blast spell, the caster picks a creature at its center for the purposes of the Morph Feat — this doesn't prevent the spell from being cast if no creatures are present. If Morph is used to remove the Blast quality, the spell still affects whichever creature was at its center.

Curse — a spell with the Curse quality tends to be long lasting unless stated otherwise. Ways to remove a curse exist, but by default, one of the following must happen if the Curse has no set duration:

  • Killing the spellcaster who cast the Curse ends it
  • Using a specialist, or a specialized spell or item, to remove it

A spellcaster can only have one instance of a given Curse active at a time — but if that casting affected multiple targets through Blast or a Morph effect, all of them are included in that single instance.

1.9

Fatigue

A Fatigued character cannot use Feats until they rest. Fatigue clears on a Short Rest — see Rest & Recovery, below.

1.10

Damage

An Attack's Damage is deducted from the target's Guard.

  • If they have at least 1 Guard remaining, they Evade the Attack.
  • If it leaves them with exactly 0 Guard, they gain a Scar.
  • If it exceeds the target's Guard, the excess is deducted from Vigor and they are Wounded.
  • If they lose half or more of their remaining Vigor, it is a Mortal Wound. Mortally Wounded targets are down and dying — they die if left untended for an hour, but can be patched up in a few moments.
  • If Damage reduces the target's Vigor to 0, they are Slain.
1.11

Scars

When a Scar is gained, take the die that caused it and roll it again, consulting the table below for the specific Scar caused. Keep track of every scar a Fatekin receives — this tally is permanent and never resets. On subsequent scar rolls, add the number of current scars to the roll. A Scar's ongoing effects clear at the end of a season, or if remedied by a specialist, but the tally itself remains. For every 3 scars a Fatekin receives, they gain one Experience.

Virtue Loss through a Scar is not Damage, so it can never Mortally Wound or Slay the victim. Scars are only gained through real, deadly combat — never through training or a bloodless duel.

1.12

Magical Scars

A Fatekin gains a Magical Scar when a spell functioning as an Attack reduces them to exactly 0 Guard, or when a spell specifically states that it inflicts one — some spells cost their own caster a Magical Scar. When a Magical Scar is gained, take the die that caused it (the highest, if several) and roll it again, consulting the table below for the specific Scar caused. A Magical Scar's ongoing effects clear at the end of a season, or if remedied by a specialist.

Virtue Loss through a Magical Scar is not Damage, so it can never Mortally Wound or Slay the victim. Magical Scars are only gained from spells — never from a mundane wound.

1.14

Inventory & Encumbrance

Every character sheet has ten slots for Items and ten slots for Spells. If a character fills their tenth Item slot, they become Encumbered — and while Encumbered, their Guard is set to 0.

Worn Equipment

Items being worn don't take up an inventory slot — unless they have the Bulky quality, in which case they take up one slot even while worn.

Four items can be worn as armor at once: a Shield, a Helm, an Underlayer (such as a Gambeson), and an Outer Layer (such as Plate).

Petty Items & Consumables

Petty items don't take up an inventory slot at all, unless a character is carrying an unreasonable amount of them.

Bombs, Flasks, Vials, Syringes, and most other consumable items store three to an inventory slot. Remedies are the exception — each one takes up a full slot on its own.

Spells in Memory

A character can hold up to ten spells in memory at any given time, and cannot memorize more than ten.

1.15

Structures & Vehicles

Structures and Vehicles reduced to 0 Guard are Wrecked, and all within suffer d6 Damage. Weapons unsuitable for causing structural Damage have their Attacks Impaired. Guard is restored by minor repairs, but Wrecked Vehicles and Structures require lengthy Specialist repair.

Colliding Vehicles both take d6 Damage to Guard. If there is a major weight difference, the heavier Vehicle gets a +d12 Bonus. Vehicles take no Damage for running over soft targets like people.

While Piloted, Feats can be used as if the Pilot were attacking in place of the Vehicle, if the Vehicle can make Attacks. Remote-piloted Vehicles cannot use Feats.

Structures and Vehicles do not have Virtues and do not make Saves. If a situation ever arises where a Vehicle might need to make a Save, the Pilot makes it using whichever Virtue makes sense — typically Grace, as the Pilot maneuvers the Vehicle to pass the Save in question.

1.16

Rest & Recovery

There are three types of Rest:

  • Short Rest — takes just a few minutes to catch your breath. Restores Guard and removes Fatigue.
  • Night's Rest — can only be taken in a safe place with proper food and lodging. Restores Vigor to full.
  • Full Rest — takes a week in a safe place with food and lodging. Restores all Virtues to full.

Remedies

Remedies also exist that restore Virtues. Each Remedy is enough for up to four Fatekin, takes an hour to use, and restores 6 points to the relevant Virtue it remedies. Typical remedies are available at specialist shops in Lund.

Stitcher-Drones

Restores Vigor

Automatons that repair bodily harm, restoring 6 Vigor to injured Fatekin.

Stimulant-Rigs

Restores Grace

Sets of brewing equipment, stimulating powders, and elixirs that restore 6 Grace to weary Fatekin.

Sedative-Assortment

Restores Spirit

A set of specialized sedative medicines that, when imbibed, restore 6 Spirit to dejected Fatekin.

Impassioned

Impassioned is a special resource awarded to a Fatekin who indulges their Passion (defined at character creation), roleplays exceptionally well, or does something clever the GM appreciates.

A Fatekin may expend a use of Impassioned to automatically pass any Virtue Save that would otherwise cause Fatigue from using a Feat, or to immediately restore 1d6 Spirit.

GM Note

A Night's Rest or Full Rest taken outside a warded space is never fully safe in Lund — see Chapter IV for what tends to notice.

1.17

Advancement

There are three levels of play in this system. A Fatekin grows more capable both through choices made at character creation and through advancement during play.

Starting at Higher Levels

Not every campaign begins at Level 1. If a story calls for more seasoned Fatekin from the outset, use the table below in place of the standard Level 1 method.

LevelVirtue RollsGuard RollBonus
11d6 + 1d12 (×3, assign freely)1d6—
23d6 (×3, assign freely)2d6+1 to any 2 Virtues
32d6 + 5 (×3, assign freely)2d6 + 6+2 to any 2 Virtues

At every level, three separate rolls are made for the Virtue pool — a Fatekin assigns each result to Vigor, Grace, or Spirit as they choose, rather than rolling each Virtue in a fixed order.

Leveling Up

When a Fatekin advances to Level 2:

  • Draw three cards from the Minor Arcana and keep one.
  • Roll 2d6 for Guard — if the result is higher than their current Guard, it replaces it.
  • Add 1 point to any two Virtues.

When a Fatekin advances to Level 3:

  • Draw three cards from the Major Arcana and keep one.
  • Roll 3d6 for Guard — if the result is higher than their current Guard, it replaces it.
  • Add 2 points to any two Virtues.

Kept Fate cards represent boons, omens, or bonds carried forward — see Chapter V's Fate Draw for how a card's Spell, Gift, and Starting Gear are read.
